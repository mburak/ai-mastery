Most of what I write here assumes AI is the default. Spec it, prompt it, review it, ship it. The cycle works.

<!--more-->

There are still things I don't hand off.

**Small changes.** A typo, a rename, a one-line edit. The whole loop — describe, wait, read the diff, accept — is heavier than just typing the change. For trivial edits, AI is overhead.

**Learning new tech.** I've been going deep on AI for months. I don't have Claude write my notes. I don't have it solve my exercises. When I'm trying to build intuition for something, typing it myself is part of the process. The model would produce a working implementation; I want to come out with a mental model. Those aren't the same output.

**Writing in my own voice.** This blog, the about page, anything I sign. AI flattens. It rounds edges. It adds adjectives. It produces clean prose that sounds like everybody else's clean prose. My CLAUDE.md has a voice rule that explicitly says "no AI-polished language." I added it after rereading a draft that had been smoothed over — every edge rounded, every sentence reasonable, the voice nowhere. The tools I love using can do that to a post in a single pass.

All three have something in common. The AI's strength — producing output efficiently — is not what I actually want.

For small changes, the goal is getting back to the task. Asking AI is a context switch.
For learning, the goal is building intuition. Asking AI skips the part that builds it.
For voice, the goal is sounding like myself. Asking AI produces everyone else's voice.

The default question I keep seeing is "should I use AI here?" Sometimes that's the wrong question. The better question is: **is producing output what I actually want?**

If yes, AI is a multiplier. If no, AI is a shortcut around the part that matters.
